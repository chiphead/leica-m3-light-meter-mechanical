# Leica M3 Light Meter — V0.6 Mechanical Architecture

Status: design baseline / not manufacturing release  
Target board: Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-1.47B, landscape orientation

## 1. Design principles

1. Establish real component and mechanism envelopes before fixing the enclosure dimensions.
2. The bottom shell carries mechanical loads. The top cover provides protection, apertures, and local guides; it must not use PCBs as structural members.
3. Give the LCD board, TSL2591, AS5600, encoder, battery, and cold-shoe assembly independent supports or retention features.
4. Keep the battery envelope configurable. Do not size the enclosure around the previous 39 x 23 x 8 mm placeholder.
5. Keep all provisional dimensions editable and label them as unverified.
6. This directory is isolated; do not move, rename, or edit unrelated firmware/project files as part of mechanical work.

## 2. Coordinate convention

Use a local assembly coordinate system:
- X: left-to-right across the landscape board; USB-C is on the left side.
- Y: front-to-rear; the TSL2591 faces the front of the meter, in front of the LCD.
- Z: vertical; LCD glass outer surface is the primary display datum.

For the display/USB relationship, define LCD glass outer surface as Z = 0. The measured USB-C metal-shell feature is the **far-side lower edge of the connector shell**, at a 7.55 mm vertical offset from the LCD glass surface. Under the convention that downward is negative Z, this feature is Z = -7.55 mm.

This is not the lower edge of the USB-C receptacle's internal opening. The enclosure aperture must be derived from the complete metal-shell envelope and its clearance.

## 3. LCD and USB-C board reference

Board: Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-1.47B, landscape, display facing upward.

Known / required:
- Board nominal envelope previously measured as approximately 37 x 21 x 8 mm; verify actual PCB and component boundaries before detailed supports.
- USB-C receptacle is on the left side.
- LCD glass outer surface should sit close to the top-cover exterior. A preliminary recess target of about 0.4 mm has been discussed; confirm against the real glass, bezel, and cover stack.
- USB-C shell far-side lower edge is 7.55 mm below the LCD glass datum (measured constraint).
- Create a real side-wall aperture. Do not use a dummy block without cutting the enclosure wall.
- Provide a plug insertion/removal envelope outside the case, not merely a receptacle-sized hole.
- Support and retain the PCB from the bottom shell or a dedicated carrier. Do not let the connector shell or LCD glass carry insertion forces.
- Avoid a tall locating wall immediately beside the connector. Use a locating edge or retainer on the opposite side and support points under mechanically safe PCB areas.

### Provisional USB-C shell envelope

Use editable placeholders until the exact connector is measured:
- metal shell width: 9.0 mm (provisional)
- metal shell height: 3.2 mm (provisional)
- shell depth: 3.5 mm (provisional)
- nominal enclosure clearance: 0.3 mm per side (provisional)
- initial rectangular aperture: 9.6 x 3.8 mm (derived from width/height plus 0.3 mm per side)

These are layout assumptions, not universal USB-C dimensions and not verified dimensions of the Waveshare connector. The 7.55 mm measured relationship remains the positioning datum. The aperture's lower edge is not automatically the shell's lower edge; derive the opening from the full shell profile.

## 4. LCD cover interface

- Create a display window in the top cover.
- Position the glass surface close to the cover exterior; preliminary target recess is 0.4 mm, subject to actual stack-up.
- Use an inner locating shoulder or removable bezel/retainer.
- Support the PCB independently from the bottom shell.
- Removing the top cover must not pull the LCD PCB out or leave it unsupported.
- Do not preserve the old V0.5 arrangement in which the display surface was approximately 9.4–10 mm below the cover exterior.

## 5. TSL2591 optical module

- Mount in front of the LCD, facing the front of the meter.
- Use a dedicated bracket and a real front-wall optical aperture.
- Add a short baffled optical tunnel to reduce stray light.
- Do not assume a generic dark plastic window is suitable: the TSL2591 responds to visible and infrared light. Select window material only after checking spectral transmission or validating its effect experimentally.
- Keep the sensor mechanically and thermally separated from the main heat-spreading block where practical.
- Its PCB is not a structural support for the enclosure.

## 6. AS5600 and magnet

The AS5600 chip/package face points **downward**, toward the magnet below it.

Stack:
1. AS5600 PCB, horizontal and independently supported.
2. Sensor package on the underside of the PCB.
3. Adjustable axial air gap.
4. Magnet on the upper face of the rotating magnet carrier.

Requirements:
- Sensor package center, magnet center, and rotation axis must be coaxial.
- Measure the gap from the actual sensor package face to the magnet surface, not from the PCB surface.
- The previous V0.5 model placed its sensor marker on the wrong side and showed an approximately 9 mm separation; do not reuse that geometry.
- Magnet pocket/shoulder should prevent lateral movement; adhesive is supplementary retention, not the only locating feature.
- Verify magnet diameter, thickness, magnetization direction, and field strength against the AS5600 operating requirements.
- Keep ferromagnetic screws, steel shafts, and magnetic materials away from the sensing field unless their effect is tested.

## 7. Bottom rotary linkage

Preserve the agreed **single-piece stepped rotor** concept:
1. External operating disc carrying the camera linkage rod.
2. Shaft journal passing through the enclosure.
3. Internal retaining flange larger than the enclosure opening.
4. Upper magnet carrier disc.

Additional parts remain separate:
- replaceable bushing/sleeve in the enclosure bore
- thrust washer
- retaining cap or retainer

The rotor must remain free to rotate. The retainer must limit axial movement without clamping the rotor. Set radial clearance through the bushing and axial endplay through the washer/retainer stack.

The Ø1 mm linkage rod is oriented vertically and attaches at the edge of the external rotor disc. Its attachment must be real geometry, not a disconnected visual cylinder. Prefer non-ferromagnetic rod material near the AS5600; verify magnetic influence if the final material is uncertain.

The old V0.5 dimensions (external disc Ø20 x 2, journal Ø10 x 2, flange Ø24 x 2, magnet carrier Ø20 x 5, enclosure bore Ø10.5) are concept placeholders only and require redesign around a real bushing and support stack.

## 8. Cold-shoe and heat-spreading module

Use a commercially available metal cold-shoe male foot as the camera interface. Treat it as a replaceable module rather than integrating an unverified shoe profile into the printed shell.

Suggested assembly:
- commercial metal cold-shoe foot
- separate aluminum heat-spreader / mounting block
- electrically insulating thermal pad between the electronics heat source and metal spreader where required
- mechanical fasteners tying the shoe and spreader into a reinforced bottom-shell load path

Constraints:
- Do not press a large metal block directly against the PCB.
- Identify the actual heat source before placing a thermal pad.
- Keep the PCB antenna region clear.
- Avoid shorts to exposed pads, shields, or connector contacts.
- Do not route cold-shoe fasteners through the AS5600 or LCD PCB.
- Keep the heat spreader away from the TSL2591 optical region where possible.
- The shoe-to-camera interface may dominate thermal resistance. Treat heat export as a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed cooling performance claim.
- Provide temperature measurement locations near the electronics heat source and on the metal shoe/spreader for comparative testing.

## 9. Top encoder and independent center button

The center button is a separate mechanism, not the encoder's integrated push switch.

Independent mechanical paths:
- encoder shaft drives the rotating dial through a hub/coupler
- center button cap and plunger do not rotate with the dial
- plunger has its own guide, return spring, travel stop, and switch actuator
- button force is reacted by a dedicated support/bracket, not by the encoder PCB or switch body alone
- keep an annular clearance between the stationary button cap/plunger and rotating dial

A preliminary button travel range of 0.5–0.8 mm may be used for layout only. Final travel and stop position must match the selected switch's operating travel and safe overtravel.

Do not assume the encoder shaft is hollow. The existing concept model used a solid Ø4 mm shaft placeholder. Keep the encoder/hub interface replaceable until the actual encoder part number, shaft diameter, shaft type, shaft length, and mounting geometry are known. A hollow-shaft encoder or a concentric sleeve arrangement is a design option, not a verified fit.

## 10. Battery and internal packaging

- Battery size is intentionally not fixed at this stage.
- Remove the old fixed 39 x 23 x 8 mm battery placeholder from the enclosure-driving constraints.
- Reserve a flexible region and cable/connector routing corridor.
- Add a battery carrier only after selecting a real cell.
- Do not place the battery directly under a PCB where it could be crushed or become a structural spacer.

## 11. Enclosure and assembly

- Initial printed-shell wall thickness: 1.6–2.0 mm, subject to material and print process.
- Use local bosses, ribs, supports, and brackets only where needed; do not fill the entire interior with solid blocks.
- Prefer screw-fastened top and bottom shells for the first prototype.
- USB-C, display, optical window, rotary shaft, and encoder openings must be real cuts.
- Provide tool access and a credible assembly/disassembly sequence.
- Check cable bend radius, plug insertion, rotor clearance, button travel, fastener access, and serviceability.
- Do not let the top cover clamp PCBs or transfer user button/rotary forces into sensor boards.

## 12. Known V0.5 defects to avoid

V0.5 is a layout/envelope concept, not a printable enclosure. Its known issues include:
- display glass surface approximately 9.4–10 mm below the top-cover exterior
- USB-C is represented by a placeholder block; left wall has no functional aperture
- TSL2591 window is only a reference block, not a cut opening or baffled tunnel
- AS5600 package marker is on the wrong side for the now-confirmed chip-down orientation
- approximately 9 mm sensor-to-magnet spacing in that incorrect orientation
- bottom rotor lacks a bushing, thrust washer, and retainer
- linkage rod is not mechanically connected to the rotor
- encoder shaft and operating dial are not mechanically coupled
- encoder bracket has no proper mounting holes
- top cover lacks the required mounting and display-retention details

## 13. V0.6 acceptance checklist

- [ ] USB-C aperture is cut through the left wall and aligns with the shell envelope.
- [ ] A representative USB-C plug can be inserted and removed without shell collision.
- [ ] LCD glass is close to the top-cover exterior and retained independently of the PCB support.
- [ ] TSL2591 has a real optical opening and baffled path.
- [ ] AS5600 chip faces downward and is coaxial with the magnet.
- [ ] Magnet-to-package gap is explicitly dimensioned and adjustable.
- [ ] Rotor turns freely with controlled radial and axial play.
- [ ] Rotor is retained against axial pull-out.
- [ ] Linkage rod is mechanically attached and its magnetic effect is considered.
- [ ] Cold-shoe foot has an independent mechanical load path.
- [ ] Heat spreader does not short or mechanically load the PCB.
- [ ] Encoder dial rotation and center-button press are mechanically independent.
- [ ] Battery remains a replaceable, non-envelope-driving module.
- [ ] All fasteners are accessible and assembly/disassembly order is feasible.
- [ ] No unrelated firmware or project files are modified.
