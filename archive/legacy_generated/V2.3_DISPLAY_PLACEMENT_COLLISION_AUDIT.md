# V2.3 display placement feasibility and collision audit

## Scope

V2.3 is a stricter feasibility review of the proposed left-wall display placement (Candidate A). It corrects the simplified vertical-clearance conclusion in V2.2 by including the AS5600 package and the V2.0 rotor's actual envelope. It is an analytical review of source dimensions only; it does not modify the V2.0 macro.

## Coordinate source verified

The V0.6 macro confirms:
- Board local origin is the lower-left corner in XY.
- Board envelope is X=0..37, Y=0..21.
- USB shell center is at local Y=BOARD_Y/2=10.5 mm.
- USB shell spans local Y=6.0..15.0 mm.
- Shell X spans -3.5..0 mm relative to the board's left edge.
- Shell Z spans -7.55..-4.35 mm from the LCD glass outer-face datum Z=0.
- Board body spans Z=-8.6..-0.6 mm.

The Candidate A translation used in V2.2 (board local origin X=-36, Y=-14.5) therefore yields:
- Board: X=-36..1, Y=-14.5..6.5, Z=-8.6..-0.6.
- USB shell: X=-39.5..-36, Y=-8.5..0.5, Z=-7.55..-4.35.
- Shell center Y=-4.0 mm; shell center Z=-5.95 mm.

These are coordinate translations of provisional V0.6 envelopes, not measured connector dimensions.

## Collision 1 — display PCB versus AS5600 package

V2.0 AS5600 package envelope:
- XY: X=-1..1, Y=-1..1.
- Z: -1.5..0.

Candidate A display board:
- XY: X=-36..1, Y=-14.5..6.5.
- Z: -8.6..-0.6.

The AS5600 package lies entirely within the display board's XY footprint. Their Z intervals overlap from -1.5 to -0.6 mm, a 0.9 mm intersection. This is a direct solid-envelope collision in the current simplified model.

The V2.2 statement that the display board and AS5600 board have a nominal 0.6 mm vertical separation only compares the display PCB body to the AS5600 PCB laminate. It omitted the downward-facing AS5600 package, which extends below the AS5600 PCB. That earlier conclusion is insufficient and is superseded by this V2.3 result.

## Collision 2 — display PCB versus bottom rotor

V2.0 rotor carrier:
- Center axis: (0,0).
- Radius: 10 mm.
- Z: -6.5..-4.5 mm.

Candidate A display board XY rectangle X=-36..1, Y=-14.5..6.5 intersects the carrier's circular XY footprint. The display board also spans Z=-8.6..-0.6, which fully contains the carrier's Z interval. Therefore, the simplified display-board envelope intersects the rotor carrier volume.

The rotor flange is Ø24 mm and occupies approximately Z=-8.4..-6.4 mm; it also intersects the display-board Z interval and its XY footprint. The journal and external disc must also be checked, but the carrier collision alone rejects Candidate A.

This is a static overlap of the modeled envelope; it is not a dynamic sweep simulation. It is sufficient to reject the current placement.

## Collision 3 — display PCB versus chassis floor

V2.0 floor:
- XY: X=-38..58, Y=-29..32.
- Z: -10.3..-8.3.

Candidate A display board Z=-8.6..-0.6. The Z overlap is 0.3 mm wherever their XY footprints overlap. Since the board lies within the floor XY envelope, the simplified solids intersect.

A local floor pocket might remove this one intersection, but it would not resolve the AS5600 package or rotor collisions. Therefore a floor pocket is not a sufficient fix.

## Consequence

**Candidate A is rejected.** It aligns the USB connector with the left wall but puts the display board through the central rotor and AS5600 package. It must not be used as the basis for a new CAD macro.

This also means the current V2.0 component placement is not a coherent 3D assembly under the V0.6 glass-face Z datum. The conflict is not limited to the LCD support pads.

## V2.4 layout work required

The next useful step is a 2D packaging study before another 3D enclosure macro:

1. Preserve the real functional relationships: TSL2591 at the front, display visible in landscape, AS5600 directly above the magnet, rotor axis and linkage path fixed by the Leica dial mechanism, and E8H7 wheel accessible from its intended side.
2. Treat the display board, AS5600 PCB/package, rotor, TSL2591 PCB and E8H7 as separate XY footprints with keep-out margins, not just nominal board rectangles.
3. Evaluate alternative display placements/orientations and show all collisions before selecting one. Do not assume a 180-degree rotation solves the issue; it reverses the USB side and changes the viewing/optical relationship.
4. Decide whether the display can be offset to a peripheral region or whether another component/axis must move. The rotor axis and camera linkage constraints should not be moved without mechanical evidence.
5. Once a collision-free XY arrangement exists, rebuild the Z stack from the LCD glass datum and component-specific measured heights.
6. Only then generate a V2.5 assembly macro with real USB wall cut, board supports and retention.

## Corrections to earlier records

- V2.2 Candidate A is superseded: its simplified board-to-AS5600 clearance conclusion omitted the AS5600 package and rotor carrier.
- V2.1's translated USB Y-coordinate was corrected in a later commit; V2.3 confirms the V0.6 source convention and final Candidate A coordinates.
- No geometry change is made to V2.0.
- No FreeCAD macro was executed. No shape validity, interference check in FreeCAD, STEP/FCStd output, physical fit, or optical validation is claimed.

**Disposition:** stop 3D enclosure detailing. Continue with a collision-aware XY packaging study.
